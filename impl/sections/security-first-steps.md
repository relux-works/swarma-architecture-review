## The rule this section applies

Every security gate binds at its **first use**, not at the first external contributor. UM7 `:401` requires the recorded class, the qualified host matrix, the task exec profile and the selected R-CEILINGS/R-EXEC/R-NET-TOOL restrictions before any child code of any managed start; UM7 `:437` applies R-EXEC to every agent or workplace task class; CB7 `:820` puts journaled R-ALLSTOP and its recovery inside the protected Claude slice; UM7 `:441` applies the standing-surface digests before the first standing launch. The external contributor is therefore the last rung of a ladder whose lower rungs already carry most of the security work. A plan that labels those rungs "later, for donors" builds S0 twice.

Each gate is proved by **positive controls and refusals together**: the permitted path must work for its admitted scope, every unadmitted path must refuse, and recovery must end in a published outcome. Refusals alone show nothing constructible; successes alone show nothing contained.

## Credential leasing and the operator boundary, in one picture

1. **Roots and keys.** The v0 root is a software key in `swarma-operator` (F-23); the operator's device key stays on the phone (CB7 `:734-742`); each agent signs as its generation-bound caller key under its own account (CB7 `:176`). Hardware custody of the root is a later root-format decision, not an S0a prerequisite. The keeper becomes the trust root at S0b (CB7 §17).
2. **Who may hold what.** Trusted-class local agents receive a leased token through the executor path only (UM7 `:431`, CB7 §12.1), readable by its holder, an accepted limit. Workplaces never receive a lease or a binding (CB7 `:611`). Remote workers use their own provider credentials on the donor machine (CIP-0010); nothing is stored for them on our side.
3. **How authority ends.** Revocation verdicts are narrow and published (F-8); restore is fenced until one of the two recovery outcomes is established (F-9, CB7 `:354`); delivered vendor tokens keep their vendor lifetime, which local revocation cannot erase, an accepted limit; renewal of standing authority needs the operator before the finite horizon, except an explicitly pre-authorised, bounded, non-widening re-grant within a published delegation (F-14, F-23 consequence 5, CB7 `:739`); a standing execution's lifetime confers no grant or lease lifetime, an old `not_revoked` verdict is no future permit, and an authenticated original caller whose grant lapsed may continue only an already admitted narrowing such as its own unbind, never a bind or a replacement (F-22, CB7 `:723`).
4. **Who acts.** Every widening act is authenticated and content-confirmed; every narrowing act is authenticated; both are refused inside agent sessions (DP7 `:114`, UM7 `:112`). Automatic narrowing and already-admitted service acts need no person (CB7 `:739`, `:815`). The observer account reads and never acts. Local operator and broker grants are closed sets; they are not a reason to build universal delegation or a second authority service.
5. **What a compromised piece can reach.** A prompt-injected trusted agent is bounded by its admitted grants, resources and placement: its lease, its caller key, the delegable subset of its grants and any admitted spawn or task-role authority (DP7 §3.3, TRU `:28`); it cannot mint operator authority or perform the operator-only acts (widening, enrolment, standing retirement or re-creation, key revocation and operator-reserved revocation), while an admitted issuer's revocation of its own grants keeps its published scope (CB7 `:350`); its admitted cleanup and own-allocation requests, including ordinary retirement of an allocation it owns (DP7 `:486`, `:404`), keep their defined scope and cleanup gates. A compromised bridge signs only under its own identity on the existing path; no delegated operation is implied ([f-mailbox-figure-verdicts](../../cycle3/findings/f-mailbox-figure-verdicts.md)). A compromised workplace can write its workspace, send ordinary signed letters and outcomes, and talk to its relay; its code exits only through complete R-TRANSFER output, and C is built on our side.

## Risks specific to security sequencing

1. **S0a mistaken for readiness.** The owner's MVP names Claude and Codex and says "before any donor"; the plan must repeat that no S0a evidence admits a workplace, and no S0b evidence either.
2. **Task tooling before the exec-policy relation.** An interpreter-bearing profile built before row 0.5 will be rebuilt; freeze the relation first, then ship profiles one qualified row at a time, and never let a profile's interpreter need become local execution of worker code.
3. **A reviewer harness that discovers candidate files.** The flagged-hunk acknowledgement cannot protect a reviewer whose harness loaded the candidate's hooks first; the data-only consumer (L.2) is cheaper than a strict adapter and must come first.
4. **Declared networking treated as enforcement.** Any A row resting on a cooperative profile is a false pass; enforced zero egress, the exec and temporary-area minima and the hard process count must be hard; a soft resource row is admitted only by the recorded per-host disposition.
5. **Status-only alerting that nobody declared.** A control whose purpose is to summon the operator must say in which version the operator is summoned; until then the watcher's narrowing is the only real control, and the operator-act path of S0a is not a substitute.
6. **Operator attention as an unbounded resource.** Flagged hunks, export waivers, refusal dispositions and alerts all summon the operator; A should admit one worker and one task class until the per-round operator load is measured.
7. **Formats frozen against moving decisions.** `workspace-result/1` is DRAFT; the join objects are pending; the keeper registration is gated; the non-workspace intake is unpublished. Code written against drafts is the rework the owner asked us to avoid; the freeze order is lens 2's, and this section only states which freezes each rung waits for.
